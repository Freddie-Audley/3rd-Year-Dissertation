\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:ProblemStatement}

\large{\raggedright
Variational quantum machine learning (QML) models can be represented by truncated Fourier series. While general results about how the size of the in principle accessible Fourier spectrum influence generalisation behaviour are known, the impact of structural features of the Fourier spectrum, in particular sparsity, have not been explored. The goal of this project is to explore via numerical simulation experiments whether Fourier sparsity or other properties of the Fourier spectrum can "explain" the generalisation behaviour of QML models in the sense that they lead to quantities that correlate well with the true generalisation error. As such, the project will mainly consist in identifying relevant QML architectures based on the recent literature, training those models while enforcing regularisation to preferentially obtain solutions with sparse (or otherwise structured) Fourier spectra, and then studying the correlation between Fourier spectra quantities and observed generalisation error. Additionally, the numerical QML part of the project may be complemented by considerations of the role of Fourier spectra in classical ML or by rigorous proofs of generalisation bounds from Fourier sparsity.\\
}
